# Better Than Keep Inventory (BTKI) - still in development.

## What is Better Than Keep Inventory?

Better Than Keep Inventory is a Minecraft data pack that aims to rebalance the experience of playing for casual players. It seeks to find a middle ground between the frustration of losing all items upon death and the overpowered nature of the "Keep Inventory" feature, providing a more balanced and enjoyable gameplay experience.

## Features:

- Players will retain a portion of their items upon death, rather than losing everything or keeping everything. The amount of items retained is determined the pack's difficulty setting and can be changed by the player.
- Tools, weapons, and armor will take durability damage instead of being lost entirely, the amount of damage is determined by the pack's difficulty setting and can be mitigated as they would normally be by the unbreaking enchantment.
- Your experience points will still be reduced (at much more forgiving rates), and then dropped upon death; but they will be retrievable indefinitely, giving players a penalty for dying, though not as punishing, and a reason to come back to the location of their death, which preserves the utility of the [Recovering Compass](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Recovery_Compass), and encourages exploration.

## The idea behind it

Minecraft is all about freedom and creativity; however, in survival mode, dropping all your items upon death and having a time limit to retrieve them, or else lose them forever, can be frustrating for casual players. It also discourages exploration and risk-taking, which are core aspects of the game, for both new and experienced players. Knowing this, Mojang introduced the "Keep Inventory" feature, which allows players to retain all their items upon death. While this feature is helpful for casual players, it can also make the game feel too easy and remove any sense of challenge or consequence, allowing players to take risks without any real penalty, and often use the feature as a cheap fast travel method. Inspired by other survival games I've played, as an enthusiast of the genre, I wanted to implement a system that aims to find a balance between these two extremes, by allowing players to keep some of their items upon death, while still imposing a penalty for dying. This way, players can still experience the thrill of survival and the consequences of their actions, without feeling overly punished or frustrated, creating a more engaging experience for casual players.
