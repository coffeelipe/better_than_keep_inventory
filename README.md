# Better Than Keep Inventory (BTKI) - still in development.

## What is Better Than Keep Inventory?

Better Than Keep Inventory is a Minecraft data pack that aims to rebalance the experience of playing for casual players. It seeks to find a middle ground between the frustration of losing all items upon death and the overpowered nature of the "Keep Inventory" feature, providing a more balanced and enjoyable gameplay.

## What does it do?

- After adding the data pack, players will retain a portion of their items upon death, rather than losing everything or keeping everything. The amount of items retained is determined by the pack's difficulty setting and can be changed by the player.

- Experience points will still be reduced (at much more forgiving rates) and then dropped upon death, but now there's no longer a time limit. They will be retrievable until the player dies again, giving players a reason to still come back to the location of their death, if they wish, which preserves the utility of the [Recovering Compass](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Recovery_Compass) <img src="https://minecraft.wiki/images/Recovery_Compass_JE1_BE1.gif?c0c26" width="16" alt="Recovery Compass icon"> and encourages further exploration.

- Because players will retain most of their items and XP loss will be drastically reduced, in order to maintain a sense of challenge, the few items that you do lose (chosen randomly) can not be retrieved anymore. However, to keep the game fair:
    - Tools, weapons, and armor will take durability damage instead of being lost entirely, giving players a chance to repair them; that way we don't need to worry about losing our shiny, super-cool, max-enchanted, boring-to-grind items on a random chance. The amount of damage taken is determined by the pack's difficulty setting and can be mitigated, as it would normally be, by the unbreaking enchantment.

    - There are a few items that are completely immune to loss, simply because they are uncraftble and exceptionally rare. Since it would be extremely frustrating and utterly unfair to lose said items on a random chance, players will never have to worry about losing the following items: [Wither Skeleton Skulls](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Wither_Skeleton_Skull) <img src="https://minecraft.wiki/images/Wither_Skeleton_Skull_%28S%29_JE2.png?0a739" width="22" alt="Wither Skeleton Skull icon">, [Nether Stars](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Nether_Star) <img src="https://minecraft.wiki/images/Nether_Star.gif?fb01f" width="16" alt="Nether Star icon">, [Silence Armor Trims](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Silence_Armor_Trim) <img src="https://minecraft.wiki/images/Silence_Armor_Trim_Smithing_Template_JE1_BE1.png?237fb&format=original" width="16" alt="Silence Armor Trim icon">, and the [Dragon Egg](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Dragon_Egg) <img src="https://minecraft.wiki/images/Dragon_Egg_JE5.png?07921" width="22" alt="Dragon Egg icon"> (which can only be obtained once per world).  

## The idea behind it

Minecraft is all about freedom and creativity; however, in survival mode, dropping all your items upon death and having a time limit to retrieve them, or else lose them forever, can be frustrating for casual players. It also discourages exploration and risk-taking, which are core aspects of the game, for both new and experienced players. Knowing this, Mojang introduced the "Keep Inventory" feature, which allows players to retain all their items upon death. While this feature is helpful for casual players, it can also make the game feel too easy and remove any sense of challenge or consequence, allowing players to take risks without any real penalty and often use the feature as a cheap and boring fast travel method, which makes the game less engaging.

Inspired by other survival games I've played, as an enthusiast of the genre, I wanted to implement a system that aims to find a balance between these two extremes by allowing players to keep some of their items upon death while still imposing a penalty for dying. This way, players can still experience the thrill of survival and the consequences of their actions without feeling overly punished or frustrated, creating a more engaging experience for casual players.
